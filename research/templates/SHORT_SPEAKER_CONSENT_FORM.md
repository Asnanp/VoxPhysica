## Why this study is being conducted

The study investigates how accurately statistical models can estimate a broad range of human heights from speech. Height inferred from voice is uncertain and is treated as a soft-biometric estimate, not a physical measurement or identity proof.

## What participation involves

If you take part, the researcher will:

1. assign you a random participant code and avoid placing your name in the research dataset;
2. confirm that you are at least 18 years old;
3. measure your standing height twice without shoes using the study protocol;
4. record approximately 8–12 minutes of prompted speech, sustained vowels, and natural speech;
5. record non-identifying study metadata such as language, recording device category, and session date; and
6. optionally invite you to a second recording session to study device and day-to-day variation.

## Data collected

- voice recordings;
- two standing-height measurements;
- age in years;
- optional self-described gender and primary language;
- broad device and recording-condition information; and
- a pseudonymous participant code.

Names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, government identifiers, and account usernames must be stored separately from research data and must not be entered in the model-training manifest.

## Risks

A voice can be personally recognizable and may be considered biometric or sensitive personal data. A model could also produce inaccurate height estimates or perform differently across demographic groups. The study will not use predictions to identify people, establish guilt, make employment or insurance decisions, or claim precise physical measurement.
